This prospective cohort study aims to develop an outcome-based model of resilience in individuals with first-episode psychosis by defining resilience as adaptive functioning under stressor exposure. Recruiting young adults from the EASY early intervention program in Hong Kong, the study will assess clinical characteristics, psychosocial factors, stressor exposure, and mental health outcomes across baseline and follow-up time points. It hypothesizes that modifiable psychosocial factors, such as social support, cognitive reappraisal, and emotion regulation, will significantly predict resilience. Using regression, network analysis, and machine learning, the project will identify key resilience-related factors and integrate them into a preliminary resilience calculator. The study is expected to provide a clinically meaningful and scalable framework for assessing resilience in early psychosis, with implications for personalized intervention and improved outcomes.
